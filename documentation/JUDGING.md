The Challenge

Can you build AI agents that work together, adapt to changing conditions, and make decisions
even when they disagree?
Individual agents can reason, use tools, and solve complex problems. This challenge asks what
happens when independent agents bring different capabilities, incomplete information, competing
priorities, or conflicting recommendations to the same problem.
Choose one of three levels: collaboration, adaptation, or collective decision-making. Participants can
build on AGNTCY and its coffeeAGNTCY reference application, or use any other agent harness or
multi-agent system (MAS) framework they are familiar with. No specific framework, AI model, or
platform is required.
Level 1 Build an AI Team
Mission Build or extend a multi-agent system with specialized agents that collaborate to solve a
problem.
Introduce agents with distinct capabilities. Show them exchanging information and coordinating actions
to complete a task that needs contributions from multiple areas of expertise. Extend the starting
application with meaningful new behavior.
Example projects Add shipping, quality control, or sustainability agents to a coffee supply chain; build a
campus assistant for transportation, scheduling, and events; or create a research team for gathering,
analyzing, and verifying information.
Success criteria Demonstrate distinct capabilities, meaningful communication, and a completed shared
task. Explain why multiple agents are useful and identify what your team added beyond the starting
framework or demo.
What can your team of AI agents accomplish together?

Level 2 Build an Adaptive AI Team
Mission Create agents that dynamically discover collaborators, adapt to changing conditions, and
resolve conflicting recommendations.
Move beyond a fixed workflow. Give agents different knowledge, capabilities, and priorities. They must
determine which collaborators can help and revise their plan when circumstances change. Running an
existing discovery or logistics demo alone is not sufficient; demonstrate your own adaptation scenario
and decision logic.
Example projects A coffee exchange finds alternative suppliers after a shipment fails; a disaster
response team reallocates resources as conditions change; or a campus planning system adjusts
when transportation or facilities become unavailable.
Success criteria Demonstrate dynamic discovery or capability-based selection; respond to at least
one unexpected change or failure; resolve conflicting recommendations toward a shared goal; and
explain why the system selected particular agents.
Can your AI team adapt when the original plan no longer works?

Level 3 Build a Collective Intelligence
Mission Create autonomous agents with competing objectives that deliberate, negotiate, and attempt
to reach consensus without a central decision-maker.
Use at least three autonomous agents with distinct objectives or perspectives. Give them a real conflict
to work through, rather than scripted agreement. Agents should exchange evidence, challenge
proposals, and revise their positions when new information arrives. Shared messaging or facilitation is
allowed, but no supervisor may impose the final decision.
Example projects Energy management agents balance cost, sustainability, and reliability; emergency
response agents negotiate scarce resources; research agents evaluate conflicting hypotheses; or
business agents negotiate investment across departments.
Success criteria Show genuine disagreement, a meaningful deliberation or negotiation mechanism,
and the effect of new information. Compare the collective outcome with an individual-agent or simpler
baseline using evidence from your scenario.
Consensus is not always the correct outcome. A successful system may recognize insufficient
evidence, irreconcilable objectives, or an unacceptable compromise. Explain when it should stop,
preserve disagreement, or seek human input.
Can independent AI agents make better decisions together than they can individually?

Getting Started

You do not need to build a multi-agent system from scratch. AGNTCY is an open-source Linux
Foundation project, originally launched by Cisco and collaborators, that provides infrastructure for
agent discovery, identity, messaging, and observability across frameworks and systems [1, 2].
coffeeAGNTCY is one practical starting point. Its fictional coffee enterprise demonstrates interactions
among agents through the Corto and Lungo applications. Corto provides a smaller entry point; Lungo
explores richer coffee supply-chain interactions [3, 4]. These applications illustrate infrastructure and
integration patterns; they do not by themselves satisfy all challenge levels.
Explore Run coffeeAGNTCY or your preferred framework. Observe the agents, messages, tools, and
decisions before making changes.
Extend Add useful capabilities and responsibilities. Make clear which behavior is yours and which
comes from the reference application.
Experiment Introduce a controlled change, conflicting recommendation, or competing objective
appropriate to your level. Capture the resulting interactions.
Demonstrate Use a repeatable scenario. Show the outcome and explain why the agents acted as they
did, including failures and unresolved disagreements.
For the AGNTCY path, start with the hands-on tutorial and official getting-started guide [3, 4], then use
the reference application repository [2]. The app-sdk is a reference integration and learning layer used
by coffeeAGNTCY; consult component-specific documentation for the current subsystem interfaces
[5].
Beyond the Hackathon The Internet of Cognition
Cisco Outshift’s Internet of Cognition (IoC) work explores how distributed agents and humans can
share knowledge and intent and reason collectively. It extends the question from connecting agents to
enabling shared understanding and collective decisions [9].
AGNTCY supplies interoperability building blocks. IoC provides a broader research context for
exploring collective intelligence. This challenge connects the two through experiments in collaboration,
adaptation, and disagreement; implementing a particular IoC protocol or product is not required.
In a coffee exchange, agents can first coordinate an order, then adapt when a supplier becomes
unavailable, and finally negotiate competing goals such as price, delivery, and sustainability. The useful
result is evidence of better decision-making, including when agreement should be withheld.
What happens when intelligence becomes a property of the group, as well as the individual?
